About

Historian & Digital Humanist

Tyler Gillis received an MA in History from the University of Nebraska Omaha in 2025 and a BA in History and Classics from the College of Charleston in 2022. His research focuses on the intersection of technology and History, particularly in the areas of interactivity, reception, and pedagogy across diverse learning platforms. Tyler is committed to building bridges between technology and History to ensure the continued scholarly relevance of the field. In his recent work, he has employed technological tools for spatial analysis of historical sites. Additionally, Tyler is a Marine Corps veteran, having served in the 3/3 Infantry Unit at Marine Corps Base Hawaii.

Teaching Statement

This statement articulates the pedagogical philosophy that anchors my work in the history classroom: that historical thinking is a skill to be practiced rather than a body of facts to be memorized. It demonstrates how I design learning experiences that foreground primary-source analysis, situate students as active interpreters of the past, and use digital tools to make historical inquiry tangible and participatory. It also reflects my commitment to inclusive teaching and to meeting students across a range of backgrounds and learning platforms.

Artifact 02

Signature Assignment

This signature assignment asks students to move beyond passive reading and to construct historical arguments of their own, drawing on primary sources and situating them within broader scholarly conversations. It is scaffolded to develop research literacy, evidence-based reasoning, and clear historical writing, while leaving room for students to pursue questions that matter to them. The assignment demonstrates my approach to authentic assessment—measuring not recall, but the interpretive and analytical habits that define the discipline.

Original course design. This assignment was conceived and authored from the ground up as part of an original course I designed—not adapted from an existing template.

Syllabus

This syllabus translates my teaching philosophy into a semester-long structure, sequencing readings, discussions, and assessments to build historical skills cumulatively over time. It demonstrates thoughtful course architecture: clear learning outcomes, a transparent and equitable grading framework, and a deliberate integration of digital humanities methods alongside traditional historical practice. The document reflects my attention to pacing, accessibility, and the student experience across the arc of a course.
